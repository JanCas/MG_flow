Overview

Who produces it?

China dominates primary supply. Baowu is a leading integrated group; Fugu’s many producers form a major cluster. Outside China, established supply includes RIMA, ICL Dead Sea Magnesium, Solikamsk, UKTMP and Kar Mineral. The directory separates current commercial evidence, interruptions, older evidence and downstream processors. S01 S02 S53

What do they start with?

Most covered Chinese operations, RIMA and Kar Mineral use dolomite. ICL uses Dead Sea brine-derived carnallite; chloride-fed plants also include Solikamsk and UKTMP. Recyclers start with metal scrap and purchased metal. Exact quarry contracts and numerical feed assays are often undisclosed. S02 S18 S23 S28 S33 S35 S45

How is the metal made?

Thermal producers heat dolomite into a reactive oxide mixture, then use a silicon-bearing material to release magnesium. Electrolytic producers prepare dry magnesium chloride and use electricity to separate the metal. Both routes finish with refining and casting or alloying. Recycling starts with existing metal and remelts it. S13 S21 S23 S33 S45

How much?

USGS estimates 1.1 million t of world primary production in 2025, including 950,000 t in China. ICL reports about 17,800 t of metal in 2025. Many other company figures are nameplates, old actuals, sales or mixed-material totals; a reliable current company league table cannot be constructed from the reviewed public evidence. S01 S18

Who buys it?

Evidence usually identifies sectors rather than direct ingot buyers. Documented links include Baowu component deliveries to Geely StarDrive, VREMT and ZF, UKTMP’s internal titanium production, and Magontec’s automotive scrap loop. Future project offtake and letters of intent are kept separate. S02 S32 S45 S56

How magnesium metal is produced

Three routes, three starting points

The key question is whether the incoming magnesium is chemically bound in a mineral or salt, or already present as metal. Primary extraction breaks that chemical bond. Recycling starts with existing metal. Subsequent alloying and casting can occur at the smelter or at another company; they do not create additional primary magnesium. S107 S113 S114

1. Dolomite and silicothermic reduction

Silicothermic means that silicon removes oxygen from a magnesium oxide feed. Ferrosilicon supplies the silicon; it is a consumed reagent, not simply furnace fuel. Pidgeon is the familiar version of this route, but furnace arrangements and operating cycles differ. RIMA describes its own process, and Baowu reports vertical-retort technology. S02 S23 S107

  1. Prepare and calcine the dolomite

    Dolomite contains magnesium and calcium carbonates. Crushing and heating remove carbonate as carbon dioxide, leaving a reactive mixture of magnesium oxide and calcium oxide, called dolime. SCRREEN2 describes calcination at roughly 1,000–1,300 °C; this is a route-level range, not a set point assigned to every producer. No magnesium metal has been made at this stage. S112

  2. Make the reduction charge

    The calcined material is ground, mixed with the silicon-bearing reductant and compacted into briquettes. Intimate contact between the solids helps the reaction proceed. Calcium fluoride is used as an additive in conventional Pidgeon descriptions, but its use and dosage should not be assumed at every plant. The prepared charge is therefore different from both the mined rock and the calcined oxide. S107 S112

  3. Release magnesium under vacuum

    Heating the charge makes magnesium vapor. Reduced pressure helps remove that vapor from the reacting solids and favors continued production. Calcium oxide helps bind the silicon and oxygen in a calcium-silicate residue. A simplified net reaction is: 2MgO + 2CaO + Si → 2Mg(vapor) + Ca2SiO4. This shows silicon as the active reagent; commercial ferrosilicon also brings iron into the charge. S111

  4. Condense the vapor

    The vapor reaches a cooler collection zone and deposits as solid magnesium, often called a crown. This vapor separation leaves much of the nonvolatile material behind. The crown then needs melting and finishing before sale as standard ingot or alloy. Residue remains in the reduction vessel and must be removed between batches in conventional batch operation. S107 S112

A useful experimental illustration: a 2005 Turkish study used calcined dolomite assaying 43.20% MgO and 47.46% CaO, with ferrosilicon containing 75% Si and 24% Fe, at 1,200 °C and 1 mbar (100 Pa). It varied time, reductant and additives. These are stated oxide/feed assays and laboratory conditions—not the composition of raw ore, a Kar Mineral plant specification, or a commercial recovery guarantee. S110

2. Chloride preparation and electrolysis

Electrolytic production uses a molten salt containing magnesium chloride. The brine from a lake or the carnallite from a mine is several preparation steps away from that cell feed. The starting source determines the purification and dehydration sequence. S107 S33

  1. Concentrate and separate the salts

    Evaporation and crystallization can recover magnesium-bearing salts from brine. Carnallite also contains potassium chloride and water bound in its crystals. Separation and purification manage unwanted salts and insoluble material before final dehydration. At Dead Sea Magnesium, the documented chain starts with Dead Sea brine-derived carnallite within the integrated ICL system. S18 S20

  2. Remove bound water without losing chloride

    Heating hydrated MgCl2 can cause hydrolysis: water reacts with the chloride, producing magnesium hydroxychloride and eventually MgO while releasing HCl. This is why preparing dry chloride requires controlled chemistry, rather than only evaporating free water. A 2023 diffraction study directly observed the sequence. These laboratory transformations explain the challenge; their temperatures are not prescribed plant settings. S109

  3. Condition the prepared salt

    Industrial schemes can combine staged dehydration, melting, chlorination of oxide impurities and settling. The Kazakhstan reference describes this sequence for carnallite preparation. Its prepared-feed limits include MgCl2 ≥50% for grade A or ≥48% for grade B, with MgO ≤0.80% and carbon ≤0.08%. These percentages describe a salt mixture, not metal purity. S33

  4. Use electricity to separate metal and chlorine

    Direct current produces magnesium at the cathode and chlorine at the anode: MgCl2 → Mg + Cl2. Other chlorides help give the bath suitable melting and flow properties. IMA describes electrolysis at 655–720 °C, an indicative industry range. Magnesium is collected as molten metal; keeping it apart from chlorine prevents the products from reacting back together. S107 S33

  5. Withdraw and finish the metal

    The collected magnesium is separated from entrained salt, refined and cast, or transferred as liquid metal to an integrated operation. Chlorine has its own recovery and consumption route. At a titanium complex, MgCl2 generated when magnesium reduces titanium chloride can return to electrolysis; the same magnesium can therefore circulate repeatedly. S32 S33

Electrolyte composition, dry-feed composition and saleable metal purity are three different analyses. A high magnesium-chloride recovery in a preparation trial is also different from recovery of magnesium metal across the whole plant. Qinghai’s 2025 trial disclosures illustrate this distinction. S80

3. From crude metal to a saleable product

Finishing has two jobs: remove unwanted material and deliver the required composition. Solid thermal crowns must first be melted; electrolytic metal arrives molten but can carry salt. In flux-based treatment, a salt mixture helps collect nonmetallic material and separate it from the melt. Settling and skimming separate cleaner metal from residues. The EPA description is a historical example, not a universal modern operating recipe. S108

The melt also needs protection from oxidation. Flux covers and protective gas systems are used, with the choice depending on the process and facility. Older technical references discuss gases that should not be assumed to describe a producer’s present practice. A current plant assessment needs its actual cover system and emissions data. S111

For alloy ingot, additions set the intended composition. For unalloyed ingot, impurity limits remain the target. Sampling, chemical acceptance and identification of the melt connect production to the buyer’s specification. UKTMP’s technical reference describes sampling and marking ingots with grade and melt identification. S33 S95

Die casting, billet extrusion, rolling, machining and powder manufacture are later product routes. These steps change shape or properties; their tonnage must not be added to the primary metal from which they started. General fabrication routes do not prove that a particular smelter supplies a particular OEM. S114

4. Recycling and the limits of remelting

  1. Keep compatible material together

    Clean diecasting returns of a known alloy are easier to return to the same specification than mixed end-of-life scrap. Sorting matters because coatings, fasteners and other metals can introduce iron, nickel or copper. Machining chips require attention to lubricants, moisture and oxidation; Luxfer’s guidance stresses the condition and segregation of this stream. S113 S51

  2. Remelt and separate unwanted material

    Recycling can use flux refining, or fluxless melting followed by settling or gas treatment. The objective is to recover usable metal while controlling inclusions and composition. Oxides and processing residues are separate from recovered alloy, so scrap charged is not the same quantity as saleable metal. S108 S113

  3. Correct the chemistry and recast

    Remelting alone does not remove every dissolved impurity. IMA describes manganese treatment for iron control, while nickel and copper may require dilution or distillation. The chosen treatment depends on the alloy and contamination; it is not automatically part of every recycler’s process. Qualified recycled alloy can re-enter a casting loop. S113

Magontec and AMACOR belong in this metal-scrap/alloy chain. Their recycled output must remain separate from new primary metal. Magnesium retained within recycled aluminum alloy is another boundary: recycling an aluminum can does not ordinarily yield a separate magnesium ingot. S45 S48 S111

Why the process changes the supply picture

For thermal plants, the feed system includes both the magnesium-bearing mineral and the reductant. A captive dolomite mine alone does not remove dependence on ferrosilicon, silicon or energy. At Yide, integrated ferrosilicon is specifically documented; elsewhere the purchasing boundary can remain undisclosed. S13

For chloride plants, the preparation section must deliver a salt that the cells can use consistently. Qinghai’s reports of impurity control and trial metal production show why a design nameplate cannot stand in for proven, sustained output. At a titanium complex, internal chloride recycling makes gross cell output especially easy to misread as fresh supply. S80 S32

Alternative feedstocks and proposed processes remain in the project directory. For example, Verde’s disclosed aluminothermic trial uses aluminum as reductant, while other projects emphasize residue upgrading or chloride production. A successful concentrate, MgO or chloride stage is progress toward metal production; it is not itself magnesium-metal output. S75 S56

Public evidence still does not establish current plant-wide recovery, energy consumption, reagent consumption or impurity-removal performance for most producers. Those values remain undisclosed here. Purity requirements & commodity flows

Purity is only one part of quality

A buyer purchases a defined material, not simply “magnesium.” Unalloyed ingot, a casting alloy, a powder and a sacrificial anode have different acceptance criteria. ASTM B92/B92M-17(2025) covers unalloyed ingot and stick for remelting; ISO 8287:2021 covers cast unalloyed magnesium made by thermal or electrolytic routes. Neither process name alone establishes the finished grade. S95 S96

Bulk assay tells how much magnesium is present. Individual impurity limits control what the remainder may contain. As a mass-fraction illustration, 99.9% Mg leaves at most 0.1%, or 1,000 ppm, for everything else combined; it does not allow 1,000 ppm of every impurity. A separate 0.001% limit corresponds to 10 ppm. These conversions explain the units, not a universal acceptance threshold.

A high-quality magnesium alloy intentionally contains other metals, so a lower Mg percentage need not mean lower quality. Nor does a high metal assay establish freedom from oxide inclusions, surface contamination or unsuitable particle size. Iron-related corrosion is documented experimentally; its effect depends on the material and environment, so no single corrosion-tolerance number is assigned to all products. S98 S95 S97 S99

A U.S. case for electrochemical magnesium

The evidence supports developing dependable domestic primary supply. Electrochemistry offers a credible route if real feedstocks can be converted into qualified metal at a competitive total cost.

The development case

The United States has an existing industrial need for magnesium but no primary production in the USGS 2025 series. Global primary supply is concentrated, while domestic recycling serves different material loops. Taken together, these facts motivate a U.S. process that converts locally available magnesium compounds into reliable, saleable metal. They establish a supply problem; they do not, by themselves, establish which new process will be cheapest. S01

Why investigate an electrochemical route?

Electrolysis uses electrical current to convert a prepared magnesium compound into metal. It can therefore shift the reduction step away from purchasing a silicon or aluminum reductant. It still needs heat, chemical preparation, suitable cell materials, metal recovery and product finishing. Cheap ore or brine does not automatically mean cheap cell feed. S111 S116

For a chloride route, the commercial precedent is strong. The challenge is to purify and prepare a consistent chloride feed, manage moisture and impurities, separate metal from chlorine, and keep the system operating reliably. For an oxide-fed route, avoiding the chloride-preparation chain is a potential advantage, but it transfers the burden to a different electrolyte, durable anode system and metal-collection process. These are distinct development choices. S21 S109 S116

The 2018 INFINIUM final report is useful evidence of both possibility and difficulty: its pure-magnesium work used oxygen-producing zirconia tubes and reported a tube campaign of up to 170 hours. Its later focus on a higher-value Mg–Nd master alloy also means that successful alloy production cannot be assumed to demonstrate commodity-ingot economics. S116

The development hypothesis is that a well-integrated electrochemical plant can combine accessible U.S. feed with reliable power and qualified output. Low emissions are conditional: electricity generation, feed preparation, reagents, direct heat and waste treatment all belong in the comparison. An electrically driven cell alone does not establish a low-carbon or zero-emissions product. S111

A domestic oxide route likewise needs an identified supply chain. The INFINIUM study used MgO as prepared feed; it does not establish a current mine-to-plant contract. Big Blue’s Baymag arrangement illustrates another boundary: metal made in the United States can still use an imported Canadian intermediate. Domestic extraction, North American sourcing and domestic metal conversion are separate claims. S116 S120

The initial customer case can rest on existing uses. USGS estimates that 2025 reported U.S. primary consumption went to castings, principally automotive (69%), aluminum alloying (15%), iron and steel desulfurization (9%), and other uses (7%). These shares apply to that reported primary-consumption series, not to all recycled magnesium or all U.S. magnesium-containing products. They do not require a speculative magnesium-battery market to justify investigation. S01

A casting-alloy buyer needs consistent alloy chemistry and impurity control; an aluminum producer needs a reliable alloying addition; a steel-treatment customer may need a specified granule, powder or blend. A purity headline alone does not qualify metal for all three. Product form, composition, traceability and lot acceptance must be agreed with the intended customer, then demonstrated repeatedly. Magrathea’s Wogen partnership is evidence of a future route to market, with placement targeted up to 7,000 t/y by 2029. It is not evidence of current shipments or a guaranteed volume for an unrelated U.S. developer. The economic test is the whole plant

Compare delivered cost per kilogram of accepted product: prepared feed + electricity and direct heat + reagents and consumables + labor and maintenance + waste handling + capital recovery + freight, less only defensible coproduct credits. Cell energy and the purchase price of raw brine are incomplete cost boundaries. Historical process inventories show why preparation and utilities must stay inside the assessment. S111

Illustrative sensitivity, not observed plant performance or a power-price quote: assume total metered plant electricity of 15 kWh/kg saleable Mg and electricity at $60/MWh. Electricity alone costs 15 × 60 / 1,000 = $0.90/kg. At the same consumption, each $10/MWh change shifts that component by $0.15/kg. These analyst-selected inputs say nothing about the other costs, direct fuel, the negotiated tariff or the final metal selling price.

For a chloride plant, chlorine handling or sale needs a credible local solution; coproduct revenue should not be assumed without evidence of a buyer and usable product quality. Compare the proposed plant with an incumbent serving the same destination, product specification and contract terms. Regional price assessments have different delivery, duty, grade and lot conventions. Their differences are not automatically an available producer margin. S21 S100 S101

Environmental comparison needs the same discipline: report both direct plant performance and a feed-to-metal life-cycle boundary. Include the actual power supply, upstream reagents, direct heat, recovery losses and treatment of coproducts. A low-carbon electricity contract could improve the case, but neither an emissions advantage nor a cost advantage is demonstrated by this report. S111

A practical development objective

Current projects make the opportunity tangible while keeping its maturity in perspective. Arkansas Magnesium has feed-linked sample and future distribution evidence. Big Blue’s August 2026 construction update targets first demonstration metal in January 2027; its DOE project seeks a sustained run of an alternative thermal route. Neither is counted here as established U.S. annual primary supply. The project directory records each separately. S58 S59 S117 S118

  1. Choose feed and product together

    Identify a domestic feed supplier and an initial product specification before optimizing the cell. Establish which impurities and preparation costs control that pairing.

  2. Prove the integrated balance

    Measure feed preparation, extraction, collection, refining, accepted product and residues in one campaign. Demonstrate stability with representative feed variation.

  3. Run customer qualification alongside scale-up

    Produce repeated trial lots, obtain buyer feedback, and test the cost model using measured recovery, energy and component replacement.

  4. Advance only when the supply chain closes

    Tie a continuous demonstration to credible feed, power, waste handling and purchase commitments. Select commercial scale from the supported market and plant performance.

The proposed objective is dependable U.S. primary magnesium from a documented feed chain, with repeatable buyer-qualified output and competitive delivered cost. Electrochemical development is well motivated because it builds on a proven metal-extraction principle and can address domestic chloride or oxide resources. The decisive innovation must be demonstrated across the entire feed-to-product system.

Methods and gaps

Scope and cutoff

Research cutoff: 27 September 2026. This is a public-source desk review of major established primary producers, selected recyclers/alloy makers, and separately identified development projects. It is not a census of every Chinese smelter or every global scrap remelter. “Latest located” means latest year-qualified figure in the reviewed evidence, not a claim that no newer private or inaccessible disclosure exists.

What counts as primary metal

Primary production is new metallic magnesium extracted from mineral/brine/residue feed. Recovery from old industrial residues may be described as recycling by a developer, but is separated here from remelting already metallic scrap. Magnesium oxide, hydroxide, chloride, carbonate, potash and dolomite tonnage are excluded from metal totals. Alloy output includes other elements; shipments may include trading and inventory movements.

Avoiding double counting

Do not add a group to its subsidiaries, primary metal to the alloys subsequently made from it, a regional total to its firms, or recycled metal to primary supply. UKTMP/AVISMA internal MgCl2 regeneration can repeatedly circulate the same magnesium. Gross electrolytic output, merchant ingot and virgin primary supply are different measures. Project nameplates and inactive plant capacities are excluded from current production.

Quantity conventions

t means metric tonnes; t/y means metric tonnes per year. Numbers in source tables reported in thousand tonnes are multiplied by 1,000. Approximate values and estimates retain their labels. Annual actuals, national estimates, sales, partial-year shipments and historical capacities are shown on separate bases. Unknown values remain blank in numeric exports, never imputed as zero. USGS dashes mean zero and are displayed as such; they are different from unknown company quantities.

Feedstock analytical basis

Report Mg, MgO and MgCl2 as stated; do not silently convert among them. Raw ore/brine, dolime (calcined dolomite), prepared chloride, scrap alloy and final metal are distinct sampling boundaries. Dry/wet basis, impurities and preparation are retained when disclosed; otherwise marked unavailable. No theoretical stoichiometric ore requirement is represented as an observed industrial consumption rate.

Process names

Silicothermic means using silicon, normally supplied in ferrosilicon, to remove oxygen and release magnesium. Aluminothermic uses aluminum instead. These materials are called reductants. Pidgeon names a thermal route; VAMI identifies the electrolytic technology used at Dead Sea Magnesium. Facility-specific explanations follow the feed through preparation, metal production and finishing.

Customers and applications

A named OEM at the end of a component chain is not automatically an ingot buyer. Current deliveries, historical relationships, internal consumption, development partnerships, future offtake and nonbinding LOIs are identified separately. General industry applications are context, not a producer-specific sales allocation. S65
