The development case

The United States has an existing industrial need for magnesium but no primary production in the USGS 2025 series. Global primary supply is concentrated, while domestic recycling serves different material loops. Taken together, these facts motivate a U.S. process that converts locally available magnesium compounds into reliable, saleable metal. They establish a supply problem; they do not, by themselves, establish which new process will be cheapest. S01

This section is an engineering synthesis of the producer, feedstock, process and market evidence in this report, as of 27 September 2026. Its development recommendations are analysis, not a company feasibility study or an assertion that a proposed plant is operating. Electrolysis is already an industrial route: Dead Sea Magnesium operates chloride electrolysis, and US Magnesium used it historically. The opportunity is to improve the complete production chain for U.S. conditions. S18 S21 S01

01

Supply need

Concentrated world primary production + absent U.S. primary output → a reason to develop another dependable source.

02

Technical opportunity

Domestic brines or prepared oxide → controlled electrochemical conversion → specified metal or alloy feed.

03

Commercial test

Repeatable quality + sustained operation + competitive delivered cost → buyer qualification and contracted sales.

The measures below have different boundaries and must not be added. In particular, magnesium recovered within an aluminum alloy is not a corresponding quantity of merchant pure-magnesium ingot. Why investigate an electrochemical route?

Electrolysis uses electrical current to convert a prepared magnesium compound into metal. It can therefore shift the reduction step away from purchasing a silicon or aluminum reductant. It still needs heat, chemical preparation, suitable cell materials, metal recovery and product finishing. Cheap ore or brine does not automatically mean cheap cell feed. S111 S116

For a chloride route, the commercial precedent is strong. The challenge is to purify and prepare a consistent chloride feed, manage moisture and impurities, separate metal from chlorine, and keep the system operating reliably. For an oxide-fed route, avoiding the chloride-preparation chain is a potential advantage, but it transfers the burden to a different electrolyte, durable anode system and metal-collection process. These are distinct development choices. S21 S109 S116

The 2018 INFINIUM final report is useful evidence of both possibility and difficulty: its pure-magnesium work used oxygen-producing zirconia tubes and reported a tube campaign of up to 170 hours. That is research performance, not established commercial service life. Its later focus on a higher-value Mg–Nd master alloy also means that successful alloy production cannot be assumed to demonstrate commodity-ingot economics. S116

The development hypothesis is that a well-integrated electrochemical plant can combine accessible U.S. feed with reliable power and qualified output. Low emissions are conditional: electricity generation, feed preparation, reagents, direct heat and waste treatment all belong in the comparison. An electrically driven cell alone does not establish a low-carbon or zero-emissions product. S111

Match a domestic feed to a real buyer

Arkansas provides a concrete chloride-feed example. Magrathea reports magnesium above 99.9% purity from TETRA Smackover brine, and the Arkansas Magnesium joint venture was established in March 2026. Its separate San Francisco Bay pilot has a Cargill sea-salt coproduct feed arrangement. These are two disclosed supply relationships, not interchangeable sources or proof of sustained annual production. Raw-brine assays, prepared-feed impurity limits and plant-wide feed consumption remain undisclosed in the reviewed material. S58 S71 S72

A domestic oxide route likewise needs an identified supply chain. The INFINIUM study used MgO as prepared feed; it does not establish a current mine-to-plant contract. Big Blue’s Baymag arrangement illustrates another boundary: metal made in the United States can still use an imported Canadian intermediate. Domestic extraction, North American sourcing and domestic metal conversion are separate claims. S116 S120

The initial customer case can rest on existing uses. USGS estimates that 2025 reported U.S. primary consumption went to castings, principally automotive (69%), aluminum alloying (15%), iron and steel desulfurization (9%), and other uses (7%). These shares apply to that reported primary-consumption series, not to all recycled magnesium or all U.S. magnesium-containing products. They do not require a speculative magnesium-battery market to justify investigation. S01

A casting-alloy buyer needs consistent alloy chemistry and impurity control; an aluminum producer needs a reliable alloying addition; a steel-treatment customer may need a specified granule, powder or blend. A purity headline alone does not qualify metal for all three. Product form, composition, traceability and lot acceptance must be agreed with the intended customer, then demonstrated repeatedly. See the purity and commodity-flow section for published examples. S95 S97 S98 S54

Magrathea’s Wogen partnership is evidence of a future route to market, with placement targeted up to 7,000 t/y by 2029. It is not evidence of current shipments or a guaranteed volume for an unrelated U.S. developer. Named distributor relationships, nonbinding interest, product qualification and enforceable purchase commitments represent different levels of commercial evidence. S59

The economic test is the whole plant

Compare delivered cost per kilogram of accepted product: prepared feed + electricity and direct heat + reagents and consumables + labor and maintenance + waste handling + capital recovery + freight, less only defensible coproduct credits. Cell energy and the purchase price of raw brine are incomplete cost boundaries. Historical process inventories show why preparation and utilities must stay inside the assessment. S111

Illustrative sensitivity, not observed plant performance or a power-price quote: assume total metered plant electricity of 15 kWh/kg saleable Mg and electricity at $60/MWh. Electricity alone costs 15 × 60 / 1,000 = $0.90/kg. At the same consumption, each $10/MWh change shifts that component by $0.15/kg. These analyst-selected inputs say nothing about the other costs, direct fuel, the negotiated tariff or the final metal selling price.

For a chloride plant, chlorine handling or sale needs a credible local solution; coproduct revenue should not be assumed without evidence of a buyer and usable product quality. Compare the proposed plant with an incumbent serving the same destination, product specification and contract terms. Regional price assessments have different delivery, duty, grade and lot conventions. Their differences are not automatically an available producer margin. S21 S100 S101

Environmental comparison needs the same discipline: report both direct plant performance and a feed-to-metal life-cycle boundary. Include the actual power supply, upstream reagents, direct heat, recovery losses and treatment of coproducts. A low-carbon electricity contract could improve the case, but neither an emissions advantage nor a cost advantage is demonstrated by this report. S111

A practical development objective

Current projects make the opportunity tangible while keeping its maturity in perspective. Arkansas Magnesium has feed-linked sample and future distribution evidence. Big Blue’s August 2026 construction update targets first demonstration metal in January 2027; its DOE project seeks a sustained run of an alternative thermal route. Neither is counted here as established U.S. annual primary supply. The project directory records each separately. S58 S59 S117 S118

  1. Choose feed and product together

    Identify a domestic feed supplier and an initial product specification before optimizing the cell. Establish which impurities and preparation costs control that pairing.

  2. Prove the integrated balance

    Measure feed preparation, extraction, collection, refining, accepted product and residues in one campaign. Demonstrate stability with representative feed variation.

  3. Run customer qualification alongside scale-up

    Produce repeated trial lots, obtain buyer feedback, and test the cost model using measured recovery, energy and component replacement.

  4. Advance only when the supply chain closes

    Tie a continuous demonstration to credible feed, power, waste handling and purchase commitments. Select commercial scale from the supported market and plant performance.

The proposed objective is dependable U.S. primary magnesium from a documented feed chain, with repeatable buyer-qualified output and competitive delivered cost. Electrochemical development is well motivated because it builds on a proven metal-extraction principle and can address domestic chloride or oxide resources. The decisive innovation must be demonstrated across the entire feed-to-product system.
