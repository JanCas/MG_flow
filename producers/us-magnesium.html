US Magnesium

Rowley, Utah, Great Salt Lake

Company and facilities

Ownership
Former operator: US Magnesium LLC. Utah’s Division of Forestry, Fire and State Lands acquired key land, water rights and mineral rights in the bankruptcy sale. Certain fixtures and personal property were excluded; the state did not simply acquire the entire operating company. S40 S66
Main products
Historically pure magnesium and alloys

Feedstock and preparation

Starting material
Great Salt Lake brine, concentrated in company evaporation ponds; purified/dehydrated chloride is the production intermediate.
Source and purchasing
Direct lake-brine extraction and internal concentration, not purchased ore. Company describes concentration from about 0.5% Mg in lake brine to 9% Mg in pond concentrate. S42
Grade and impurities
0.5% and 9% are company-stated Mg concentrations, not MgO or MgCl2 percentages. A full contemporaneous impurity analysis and dry electrolyser-feed specification were not located. S42
Consumption and boundaries
Concentration increase is not a recovery or lake-volume/metal consumption factor. No complete plant water/salt mass balance located.
Supply dependencies
Falling lake levels historically drove canal extension to preserve brine access. After the 2026 state asset purchase, Utah offered the former property for mineral-extraction and processing redevelopment. Lake protection and redevelopment both matter; the offering does not establish a selected operator or magnesium restart. S43 S66 S87

Production process

Route at the covered facility
Concentrate brine → remove interfering constituents and prepare dry chloride → molten-salt electrolysis → refine/alloy and cast magnesium. This is the historical Rowley route; current marketing pages do not prove operation. S41 S42

Output and capacity

Actual output and reporting year
US primary metal production: none in 2024 and 2025; national evidence is consistent with the sole major plant’s inactivity. S01
Capacity, t/y
63,500 t/y legacy company nameplate; USGS rounds US capacity to 64,000 t/y. Idle capacity is not current supply. S41 S01
Interpretation and recent trend
Retained as a former major supplier and an important explanation of US supply loss, separate from operating producers.

Buyers and applications

Documented buyers / partners
No current metal customers established while production is inactive.
Intermediate products
Historical ingot/alloy; no current shipment chain verified
Final applications
Historical industry sectors only; no current named buyer inferred.
Relationship basis
Historical facility; no current delivery claim

Evidence gaps

What remains unresolved
No primary restart or awarded redevelopment operator established. Sale exclusions, equipment ownership and lease terms require reconciliation before treating legacy capacity as available supply.
