Three routes, three starting points

The key question is whether the incoming magnesium is chemically bound in a mineral or salt, or already present as metal. Primary extraction breaks that chemical bond. Recycling starts with existing metal. Subsequent alloying and casting can occur at the smelter or at another company; they do not create additional primary magnesium. S107 S113 S114

01

Concentrate and purify → prepare dry chloride → electrolyze molten salt → separate magnesium and chlorine → refine and cast or use internally. S107

Sort by alloy and contamination → prepare scrap → remelt and clean → adjust composition → cast qualified alloy for reuse. S108 S113

1. Dolomite and silicothermic reduction

Silicothermic means that silicon removes oxygen from a magnesium oxide feed. Ferrosilicon supplies the silicon; it is a consumed reagent, not simply furnace fuel. Pidgeon is the familiar version of this route, but furnace arrangements and operating cycles differ. RIMA describes its own process, and Baowu reports vertical-retort technology. S02 S23 S107

  1. Prepare and calcine the dolomite

    Dolomite contains magnesium and calcium carbonates. Crushing and heating remove carbonate as carbon dioxide, leaving a reactive mixture of magnesium oxide and calcium oxide, called dolime. SCRREEN2 describes calcination at roughly 1,000–1,300 °C; this is a route-level range, not a set point assigned to every producer. No magnesium metal has been made at this stage. S112

  2. Make the reduction charge

    The calcined material is ground, mixed with the silicon-bearing reductant and compacted into briquettes. Intimate contact between the solids helps the reaction proceed. Calcium fluoride is used as an additive in conventional Pidgeon descriptions, but its use and dosage should not be assumed at every plant. The prepared charge is therefore different from both the mined rock and the calcined oxide. S107 S112

  3. Release magnesium under vacuum

    Heating the charge makes magnesium vapor. Reduced pressure helps remove that vapor from the reacting solids and favors continued production. Calcium oxide helps bind the silicon and oxygen in a calcium-silicate residue. A simplified net reaction is: 2MgO + 2CaO + Si → 2Mg(vapor) + Ca2SiO4. This shows silicon as the active reagent; commercial ferrosilicon also brings iron into the charge. S111

  4. Condense the vapor

    The vapor reaches a cooler collection zone and deposits as solid magnesium, often called a crown. This vapor separation leaves much of the nonvolatile material behind. The crown then needs melting and finishing before sale as standard ingot or alloy. Residue remains in the reduction vessel and must be removed between batches in conventional batch operation. S107 S112

A useful experimental illustration: a 2005 Turkish study used calcined dolomite assaying 43.20% MgO and 47.46% CaO, with ferrosilicon containing 75% Si and 24% Fe, at 1,200 °C and 1 mbar (100 Pa). It varied time, reductant and additives. These are stated oxide/feed assays and laboratory conditions—not the composition of raw ore, a Kar Mineral plant specification, or a commercial recovery guarantee. S110

2. Chloride preparation and electrolysis

Electrolytic production uses a molten salt containing magnesium chloride. The brine from a lake or the carnallite from a mine is several preparation steps away from that cell feed. The starting source determines the purification and dehydration sequence. S107 S33

  1. Concentrate and separate the salts

    Evaporation and crystallization can recover magnesium-bearing salts from brine. Carnallite also contains potassium chloride and water bound in its crystals. Separation and purification manage unwanted salts and insoluble material before final dehydration. At Dead Sea Magnesium, the documented chain starts with Dead Sea brine-derived carnallite within the integrated ICL system. S18 S20

  2. Remove bound water without losing chloride

    Heating hydrated MgCl2 can cause hydrolysis: water reacts with the chloride, producing magnesium hydroxychloride and eventually MgO while releasing HCl. This is why preparing dry chloride requires controlled chemistry, rather than only evaporating free water. A 2023 diffraction study directly observed the sequence. These laboratory transformations explain the challenge; their temperatures are not prescribed plant settings. S109

  3. Condition the prepared salt

    Industrial schemes can combine staged dehydration, melting, chlorination of oxide impurities and settling. The Kazakhstan reference describes this sequence for carnallite preparation. Its prepared-feed limits include MgCl2 ≥50% for grade A or ≥48% for grade B, with MgO ≤0.80% and carbon ≤0.08%. These percentages describe a salt mixture, not metal purity. S33

  4. Use electricity to separate metal and chlorine

    Direct current produces magnesium at the cathode and chlorine at the anode: MgCl2 → Mg + Cl2. Other chlorides help give the bath suitable melting and flow properties. IMA describes electrolysis at 655–720 °C, an indicative industry range. Magnesium is collected as molten metal; keeping it apart from chlorine prevents the products from reacting back together. S107 S33

  5. Withdraw and finish the metal

    The collected magnesium is separated from entrained salt, refined and cast, or transferred as liquid metal to an integrated operation. Chlorine has its own recovery and consumption route. At a titanium complex, MgCl2 generated when magnesium reduces titanium chloride can return to electrolysis; the same magnesium can therefore circulate repeatedly. S32 S33

Electrolyte composition, dry-feed composition and saleable metal purity are three different analyses. A high magnesium-chloride recovery in a preparation trial is also different from recovery of magnesium metal across the whole plant. Qinghai’s 2025 trial disclosures illustrate this distinction. S80

3. From crude metal to a saleable product

Finishing has two jobs: remove unwanted material and deliver the required composition. Solid thermal crowns must first be melted; electrolytic metal arrives molten but can carry salt. In flux-based treatment, a salt mixture helps collect nonmetallic material and separate it from the melt. Settling and skimming separate cleaner metal from residues. The EPA description is a historical example, not a universal modern operating recipe. S108

The melt also needs protection from oxidation. Flux covers and protective gas systems are used, with the choice depending on the process and facility. Older technical references discuss gases that should not be assumed to describe a producer’s present practice. A current plant assessment needs its actual cover system and emissions data. S111

For alloy ingot, additions set the intended composition. For unalloyed ingot, impurity limits remain the target. Sampling, chemical acceptance and identification of the melt connect production to the buyer’s specification. UKTMP’s technical reference describes sampling and marking ingots with grade and melt identification. S33 S95

Die casting, billet extrusion, rolling, machining and powder manufacture are later product routes. These steps change shape or properties; their tonnage must not be added to the primary metal from which they started. General fabrication routes do not prove that a particular smelter supplies a particular OEM. S114

4. Recycling and the limits of remelting

  1. Keep compatible material together

    Clean diecasting returns of a known alloy are easier to return to the same specification than mixed end-of-life scrap. Sorting matters because coatings, fasteners and other metals can introduce iron, nickel or copper. Machining chips require attention to lubricants, moisture and oxidation; Luxfer’s guidance stresses the condition and segregation of this stream. S113 S51

  2. Remelt and separate unwanted material

    Recycling can use flux refining, or fluxless melting followed by settling or gas treatment. The objective is to recover usable metal while controlling inclusions and composition. Oxides and processing residues are separate from recovered alloy, so scrap charged is not the same quantity as saleable metal. S108 S113

  3. Correct the chemistry and recast

    Remelting alone does not remove every dissolved impurity. IMA describes manganese treatment for iron control, while nickel and copper may require dilution or distillation. The chosen treatment depends on the alloy and contamination; it is not automatically part of every recycler’s process. Qualified recycled alloy can re-enter a casting loop. S113

Magontec and AMACOR belong in this metal-scrap/alloy chain. Their recycled output must remain separate from new primary metal. Magnesium retained within recycled aluminum alloy is another boundary: recycling an aluminum can does not ordinarily yield a separate magnesium ingot. S45 S48 S111

Reading feed and energy numbers correctly

A feed-to-product ratio needs both ends named. Mined dolomite, calcined dolime and briquetted charge have different masses. Likewise, a tonne of hydrated carnallite is not a tonne of anhydrous MgCl2. Some reported ratios describe a mixture recipe; others describe a recovery percentage or a plant-wide material inventory. They cannot be substituted for one another.

Energy comparisons must include the same stages. A cell-only electricity number omits chloride preparation and finishing. A thermal plant’s purchased electricity can omit the fuel used for heat and the electricity embodied in purchased ferrosilicon. Argonne explicitly identifies incompatible boundaries in older datasets; those figures do not support a current ranking of producer efficiency. S111

Why the process changes the supply picture

For thermal plants, the feed system includes both the magnesium-bearing mineral and the reductant. A captive dolomite mine alone does not remove dependence on ferrosilicon, silicon or energy. At Yide, integrated ferrosilicon is specifically documented; elsewhere the purchasing boundary can remain undisclosed. S13

For chloride plants, the preparation section must deliver a salt that the cells can use consistently. Qinghai’s reports of impurity control and trial metal production show why a design nameplate cannot stand in for proven, sustained output. At a titanium complex, internal chloride recycling makes gross cell output especially easy to misread as fresh supply. S80 S32

Alternative feedstocks and proposed processes remain in the project directory. For example, Verde’s disclosed aluminothermic trial uses aluminum as reductant, while other projects emphasize residue upgrading or chloride production. A successful concentrate, MgO or chloride stage is progress toward metal production; it is not itself magnesium-metal output. S75 S56

Public evidence still does not establish current plant-wide recovery, energy consumption, reagent consumption or impurity-removal performance for most producers. Those values remain undisclosed here. The deeper process description explains the disclosed route without turning a generic flowsheet into an audited plant balance.
